EMPTY LEG FLIGHTS

Private jet empty leg flights

An empty leg is a flight an aircraft is already making without passengers — repositioning after a charter, or returning to base. Sold at a reduced rate, with less flexibility than booking the route from scratch.

Why they're cheaper

The aircraft is flying that route regardless. Filling the seats offsets a cost the operator would otherwise absorb, so the price reflects that, not the full charter rate.

Fixed route and timing

You take the route and departure window the aircraft is already flying. There's no negotiating the airport or the time, the way there is on a standard charter.

Can be cancelled

If the original charter that created the empty leg changes or is cancelled, the empty leg usually goes with it. It suits flexible plans, not fixed ones.

Short notice

Empty legs are usually confirmed a few days out at most, sometimes less. They suit travellers who can move on short notice.

Tell us your rough dates and the routes you'd consider, and we'll flag suitable empty legs as operators release them. Nothing is published on a fixed schedule — availability changes by the day.

Common questions

How much cheaper is an empty leg?

It varies by route and operator. We don't publish a fixed discount because it isn't one — each empty leg is priced on its own terms.

Can I change the date once booked?

Usually not, or only within narrow limits. The date is fixed to the original charter creating the leg.

SAFETY & VETTING

How we vet an operator before it reaches you

We do not own the aircraft, which means checking who does is the most important thing we do. Every operator behind an option we send you has been through the same checks first.

BEFORE AN OPERATOR IS EVER OFFERED
01
Licence and certification

We confirm the operator holds a valid Air Operator Certificate for the aircraft and route in question, issued by the relevant civil aviation authority.

02
Independent safety audits

We check whether the operator holds a current rating from an independent aviation safety auditor, such as ARGUS or IS-BAO, and review when it was last renewed.

03
Insurance

We confirm the operator carries liability insurance appropriate to the aircraft type and the flight, before it is offered as an option.

04
Aircraft age and maintenance

We ask when the specific aircraft was last inspected and what maintenance programme it sits under, not just what category it belongs to.

05
Crew experience

We ask about crew hours and experience on the specific aircraft type, particularly for longer or more demanding sectors.

06
Ongoing review

Operators are not checked once and forgotten. Certifications and ratings are re-confirmed on a regular basis, not only when a booking is made.

What you can ask us

Which operator is flying this specific aircraft, and under which licence?
Does the operator hold an independent safety rating, and when was it last renewed?
How experienced is the crew on this aircraft type?

We are a broker, not a regulator. Final responsibility for the aircraft's airworthiness and the flight itself sits with the operator and its licensing authority. Our role is to check before we recommend, ask the questions above on your behalf, and tell you plainly if something does not check out.
